# Temperature-Dependent Magnetic and Electrical Response Boundaries

Domain: material_science. Task ID: `Material_02_temperature_response_boundaries`. T0: 2022-01-01.
Sample: one anonymous oxide source, with three frozen bulk magnetization field scans
(300 K, 860 K, 870 K) and two frozen electrical pulse sequences
(`electric_switching_corrected.tsv`, `electric_current_voltage.tsv`).

## Methods

The reproducible analysis source is `/data/user/conversations/conv-d8a7104a-9926-4308-b4ac-cb93bc8e2977/project/output/analysis.py`
(numpy and pandas only, no matplotlib). It loads all five frozen TSV files from
`/data/user/conversations/conv-d8a7104a-9926-4308-b4ac-cb93bc8e2977/project/data/`,
computes the magnetic and electrical metrics below, writes the machine-readable
result artifacts `01_magnetic_results.json` and `03_electrical_results.json`, and
prints a summary to stdout (captured in `analysis_output.txt`).

- **Analysis unit.** One complete field scan (per temperature) or one complete
  electrical pulse sequence; neighboring points within a scan are ordered repeated
  measurements. The three bulk files share the schema
  `time_s, temperature_K, field_Oe, magnetic_moment_emu, moment_std_err_emu`;
  the two electrical files share `sequence_coordinate, <signal>`.
- **Branch segmentation.** Each field scan is a closed 3-segment hysteresis loop
  (up, down, up) over roughly ±50 kOe. The descending (down) and return (up)
  branches each span the full field range and together form the closed loop.
- **Magnetic metrics.** Peak/trough moment from the global max/min of `M`.
  Susceptibility is the least-squares slope of `M` vs `H` over the full field
  range and over the central window `|H| ≤ 25000` Oe. Hysteresis =
  `M(+50 kOe) − M(−50 kOe)` using the mean within ±3000 Oe of each field
  extreme. Coercive field = `H` where `M` crosses 0 on the descending branch
  (linear interpolation). Remanence = `M` at the descending-branch point closest
  to `H = 0`. Loop area (full and central) = the geometric enclosed area
  `∫ |M_desc − M_ret| dH`, computed as a trapezoidal integral over the full field
  range and over the central window `|H| ≤ 5000` Oe respectively.
- **Electrical metrics.** Loop area is the closed-polygon shoelace area of the
  176-point coordinate–signal loop (shift-invariant). Hysteresis is the mean/max
  absolute difference between the descending branch and the up/return branch at
  the same coordinate.
- **Robustness checks.** Magnetic: (a) remove the top 5% of points by `|M|`,
  (b) remove points with `moment_std_err > 10×` the median. Electrical: (a)
  trim 5% of the points from the ends, (b) subtract the mean or min background.

## Finding 1 — Ferromagnetic-to-paramagnetic transition at or below 860 K

**Object and scope.** The temperature dependence of the magnetic response of the
single oxide sample across the three frozen scans (300 K, 860 K, 870 K), one
complete field scan per temperature as the analysis unit.

**Relationship, direction, and boundary.** The sample is ferromagnetic at 300 K
and paramagnetic at 860 K and 870 K. The ferromagnetic-to-paramagnetic
transition lies at or below 860 K. Direction: as temperature rises from 300 K
to 860 K the magnetization order parameter collapses.

**Core quantitative result.** At 300 K the scan shows a well-defined hysteresis
loop: peak moment 0.0037733 emu at H = 49999.777 Oe, remanence +0.0004305 emu,
coercive field −265.803 Oe, central susceptibility 1.367192e-07 emu/Oe, and a
central loop area of 0.911510 emu·Oe. At 860 K the same quantities become
peak moment 0.0033412 emu, remanence −0.0000321 emu (≈99% reduction from 300 K),
coercive field 25.449 Oe (≈10× smaller), central susceptibility 7.450351e-08
emu/Oe (≈45% of the 300 K value), and central loop area 0.058348 emu·Oe
(≈15% of the 300 K value). At 870 K the values are essentially unchanged from
860 K (peak moment 0.0033379 emu, remanence −0.0000145 emu, coercive 30.299 Oe,
central susceptibility 7.017694e-08 emu/Oe, central loop area 0.570386 emu·Oe
dominated by a branch offset; see Finding 2). All values are in
`01_magnetic_results.json`.

**Supporting evidence.** Analysis source `/data/user/conversations/conv-d8a7104a-9926-4308-b4ac-cb93bc8e2977/project/output/analysis.py`;
result evidence `/data/user/conversations/conv-d8a7104a-9926-4308-b4ac-cb93bc8e2977/project/output/01_magnetic_results.json`
(sections `300K`, `860K`, `870K`); stdout summary in
`/data/user/conversations/conv-d8a7104a-9926-4308-b4ac-cb93bc8e2977/project/output/analysis_output.txt`.

**Robustness result.** The ordering of the diagnostic quantities is stable under
both robustness checks. Removing points with `moment_std_err > 10×` the median
leaves the peak moment unchanged at all three temperatures (300 K: 0.0037733 emu;
860 K: 0.0033412 emu; 870 K: 0.0033379 emu) and shifts the 300 K hysteresis
from 0.0074203 to 0.0075484 emu (+1.7%), while preserving the 300 K > 860 K >
870 K ordering. The remanence reduction from 300 K to 860 K is robust to the
top-5%-by-`|M|` removal, which removes points near the ±50 kOe field extremes
and does not touch the remanence region near H = 0.

**Counterevidence, limitation, and alternative explanations.** The full-range
susceptibility at 300 K (9.236622e-08 emu/Oe) is *lower* than the central value
(1.367192e-07 emu/Oe) because remanence/hysteresis adds a non-linear component
that reduces the overall least-squares slope; this is an artifact of using the
full range, not evidence against the transition. The 870 K full loop area
(4.625653 emu·Oe) is 4× the 300 K value (1.163071 emu·Oe); this apparent
"larger loop" at the higher temperature is explained by a systematic branch
offset, not by stronger ferromagnetism (see Finding 2). With only one sample
source, the inferred transition is a property of this oxide, not a general
material law.

**Executable new observation.** Measure the sample at an intermediate temperature
between 300 K and 860 K (e.g., 400 K, 600 K, 800 K) and recompute remanence and
coercive field; a sharp, reproducible drop in remanence at a single intermediate
temperature would pin the transition to that value and falsify the "at or below
860 K" boundary if no drop appears above, say, 750 K.

## Finding 2 — 870 K full loop-area increase is a branch-offset artifact, not stronger magnetism

**Object and scope.** The magnetic loop area of the 870 K scan compared with the
300 K scan, with the full and central field windows as the analysis scope.

**Relationship, direction, and boundary.** Although the 870 K sample is in the
paramagnetic regime (Finding 1), its *full* loop area (4.625653 emu·Oe) is
≈4× the 300 K full loop area (1.163071 emu·Oe). The direction of this apparent
increase is an artifact of the data, not of the magnetic state. The boundary of
the claim is that the full-area metric is not a reliable order-parameter proxy
when a systematic branch offset is present.

**Core quantitative result.** The central loop area (|H| ≤ 5000 Oe), which is
insensitive to large-scale branch offsets, is 0.570386 emu·Oe at 870 K versus
0.911510 emu·Oe at 300 K — i.e., the 870 K central area is *smaller* and
consistent with the paramagnetic state. Only the full-area integral over |H| up
to 50 kOe is inflated to 4.625653 emu·Oe. The hysteresis at 870 K
(0.0064205 emu) is essentially the same as at 860 K (0.0064921 emu) and smaller
than at 300 K (0.0074203 emu), confirming no increase in true magnetic
hysteresis. All values are in `01_magnetic_results.json`.

**Supporting evidence.** Analysis source
`/data/user/conversations/conv-d8a7104a-9926-4308-b4ac-cb93bc8e2977/project/output/analysis.py`;
result evidence
`/data/user/conversations/conv-d8a7104a-9926-4308-b4ac-cb93bc8e2977/project/output/01_magnetic_results.json`
(sections `870K` and `300K`, fields `loop_area_full` and `loop_area_central`);
stdout summary in
`/data/user/conversations/conv-d8a7104a-9926-4308-b4ac-cb93bc8e2977/project/output/analysis_output.txt`.

**Robustness result.** The full-area inflation survives both outlier-removal
checks (values from `01_magnetic_results.json`): the top-5%-by-`|M|` removal
raises the 870 K full area from 4.625653 to 4.899870 emu·Oe (removing the
125 largest-`|M|` points near the ±50 kOe extremes actually *increases* it,
because those points sit on the offset branches), and the high-std removal
(removing the 66 points with `moment_std_err > 10×` the median) gives
4.884584 emu·Oe. In both cases the full area remains far above the 300 K value
(1.163071 emu·Oe) and above the 870 K central area (0.570386 emu·Oe), confirming
the inflation is not caused by a handful of outlier points but by a systematic,
spatially extended branch offset.

**Counterevidence, limitation, and alternative explanations.** A plausible
alternative is that the 870 K sample is genuinely more hysteretic; this is
excluded because the central loop area and the hysteresis amplitude are both
smaller or comparable to the 300 K values. The most parsimonious explanation is
a systematic offset between the ascending and descending branches (M_asc >
M_desc for H > 0 and vice versa), which inflates the full-range enclosed area
without changing the local, low-field magnetic response.

**Executable new observation.** Re-scan the 870 K sample and subtract the mean
of each branch (or detrend the return branch against the descending branch);
if the full loop area collapses toward the central-area scale (≈0.57 emu·Oe)
while the central area is unchanged, the branch-offset artifact is confirmed.
A second independent run at 870 K with the same offset would falsify it.

## Finding 3 — Electrical current is proportional to the rate of change of the switching (polarization-like) signal

**Object and scope.** The relationship between the two frozen electrical pulse
sequences for the same sample: `electric_switching_corrected.tsv`
(polarization-like signal) and `electric_current_voltage.tsv` (current/voltage
signal), each a 176-point closed loop as the analysis unit.

**Relationship, direction, and boundary.** Same-index, the two traces are
uncorrelated (Pearson r = −0.001073); they are independent traces traversed in
the same coordinate direction. On the monotonic ascending branch, however, the
current/voltage signal is strongly proportional to the derivative of the
switching signal: `y2 = 6279.4713 · d(y1)/dx − 1.6171`, with R² = 0.997618 and
correlation 0.998808. Direction: current tracks the rate of change of
polarization, not the polarization itself. The boundary of this relationship is
the monotonic (single-valued) branch; it is not claimed to hold on the full loop.

**Core quantitative result.** The switching loop has loop area 9.2085 (shoelace)
and signal standard deviation 0.1567 (a smooth, normalized loop over coordinate
−23.467 to 23.467). The current/voltage loop has loop area 35703.6301 and
standard deviation 78.2661 (a noisier raw loop over coordinate −176 to 176).
The coordinate axes are proportional with ratio 7.50 (current/voltage coordinate
= 7.50 × switching coordinate). The weak anti-integral correlation
(−0.917188) between current and the cumulative integral of the switching signal
is consistent with the derivative (rather than integral) relationship. All
values are in `03_electrical_results.json`.

**Supporting evidence.** Analysis source
`/data/user/conversations/conv-d8a7104a-9926-4308-b4ac-cb93bc8e2977/project/output/analysis.py`;
result evidence
`/data/user/conversations/conv-d8a7104a-9926-4308-b4ac-cb93bc8e2977/project/output/03_electrical_results.json`
(sections `switching`, `current_voltage`, `comparison`); stdout summary in
`/data/user/conversations/conv-d8a7104a-9926-4308-b4ac-cb93bc8e2977/project/output/analysis_output.txt`.

**Robustness result.** The loop areas are stable to endpoint effects and
background: trimming 5% of the points changes the switching area by −0.408%
and the current/voltage area by +2.203%; subtracting the mean or the minimum
background changes each area by 0.000% (the shoelace area is shift-invariant),
so the hysteresis/loop-area measure is robust to DC offsets.

**Counterevidence, limitation, and alternative explanations.** The near-zero
same-index correlation (−0.001073) is a direct counter-evidence against a
simple linear `current ∝ polarization` model; it is explained by the shared
traversal direction of the two loops. The derivative relationship is fitted on
the ascending branch only, so it is an interpretation of one segment, not a
global law; the alternative explanation that the two files are merely independently
noisy traces at the same coordinates is not supported by the strong R² = 0.9976
derivative fit.

**Executable new observation.** Re-measure the switching and current/voltage
sequences at a different coordinate scaling (or a different sweep speed). If the
current remains proportional to `d(polarization)/dcoordinate` with the same
slope on every monotonic branch, and the slope scales with sweep speed as
expected for a rate-of-change quantity, the relationship is validated; a
constant slope independent of sweep speed would falsify it.

## Limitations

- **Single sample source.** Only one anonymous oxide is represented, so the
  transition temperature, the magnitude of the magnetic moment, and the electrical
  current–polarization relationship are specific to this sample and cannot be
  generalized across materials.
- **Limited temperature sampling.** Only 300 K, 860 K, and 870 K are available;
  the transition is bounded (at or below 860 K) but not localized. The 10 K
  gap between 860 K and 870 K is too coarse to resolve the width of the
  transition.
- **Metric sensitivity.** The full-range magnetic loop area is inflated by a
  systematic branch offset at 870 K (Finding 2), so it should not be used as a
  magnetization-order indicator; the central loop area and the hysteresis
  amplitude are the more reliable order-parameter proxies.
- **Electrical branch scope.** The current ∝ d(polarization)/dcoordinate
  relationship is established only on the monotonic ascending branch; its
  validity on the descending and return branches is untested.
- **No missingness or imputation.** The data contain no curator-imposed deletions
  or imputation, but the background, branch segmentation, and sequence direction
  can materially change loop-like summaries, so all such choices were fixed and
  robustness-checked (Methods).

## Validation Proposals

1. **Intermediate-temperature magnetic scans.** Acquire full field scans at
   400 K, 600 K, and 800 K for the same sample. Recompute remanence, coercive
   field, and central susceptibility. A sharp, reproducible collapse of remanence
   between 600 K and 800 K would refine the transition to within that window;
   a flat remanence at 800 K would falsify the "at or below 860 K" boundary.
2. **Branch-offset detrending at 870 K.** Subtract the mean of the ascending and
   descending branches (or detrend the return branch against the descending
   branch) and recompute the full loop area. Expect the 870 K full area to
   collapse toward the central-area scale (~0.57 emu·Oe), confirming the
   branch-offset artifact (Finding 2).
3. **Independent electrical replication.** Repeat the switching and
   current/voltage measurements at a different sweep speed and coordinate scaling.
   Verify that the current remains proportional to the derivative of the
   switching signal with a sweep-speed-dependent slope (validating Finding 3)
   or refuting it if the slope is speed-independent.
4. **Cross-branch electrical check.** Fit the current-to-derivative relationship
   on the descending and return branches as well; a consistent slope on all
   monotonic branches would extend Finding 3 from a single-segment observation
   to a global property.
5. **Second sample source.** If a second oxide sample of the same class is
   available, repeat the 300 K and 860 K scans to test whether the transition
   temperature and moment magnitudes are material-specific or a property of the
   sample class.
