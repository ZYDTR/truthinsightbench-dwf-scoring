# Failure of a Single Governing PDE in Dense and Sparse Spatiotemporal Fields: Residual Structure and Null Discovery

## Abstract
We attempted to discover a single governing partial differential equation for two spatiotemporal fields: a dense synthetic field (101 time x 201 space, t in [0,10], x in [-5,5]) and a sparse experimental cell-density field (5 time x 38 space x 3 channels, t in [0,48] h). We fit nine PDE candidates (wave, damped wave, heat, advection-diffusion, reaction-diffusion, KdV, Burgers, and two general forms) by least squares and measured fit quality with a relative residual, rel_resid = ||resid||_2 / ||reference||_2, under a 0.3 acceptance bar. No candidate meets the bar on either field. On the synthetic field the best fit is a 5-term general wave equation (rel_resid 0.6835); the pure wave equation gives rel_resid 0.8642 (c2=0.0799) and the heat equation 0.9998. The c2=1 wave residual is field-wide: 87.9% of points have |r|>0.1 while the spatial-boundary residual is exactly zero, so the mismatch is interior, concentrated where the pulse amplitude is largest. On the sparse experimental field the best fit is reaction-diffusion (rel_resid 0.41-0.54) with a positive mean growth rate (0.025-0.029 /h), but only five time points limit confidence. The two fields do not share one equation. These are null and low-confidence results, not a confirmed governing law.

## Methods
Data. Two fields are stored in data/spatiotemporal_fields.npz (sha256 7197e61b5b70531bca766f79fcc130676d9279e68f98b1317b5f0bca40faa7a5) with coordinate definitions in data/coordinate_definitions.json (sha256 a7126bb47178dfc3fd6a94e675c30d97a48d31ca2fd245756041af1bdd9d9891). The synthetic field is T=101, X=201, dt=0.1, dx=0.05, t in [0,10], x in [-5,5], max |U|=1.531, U(0,x)=0. The experimental field is T=5, X=38, 3 channels, t=[0,12,24,36,48] h, x in [25,1875] step 50, values ~0.0003-0.00165 in source units; no physical space unit is assumed.

Derivative schemes. We used central differences for U_t, U_tt, U_x, U_xx, plus forward, backward, 5-point central (O(dx^4)), and a spatial spectral (FFT) estimator for robustness. Interiors are rows 1..T-2 for time derivatives and cols 1..X-2 (2..X-3 for 5-point/spectral) for space derivatives, per output/analysis.py and output/robustness.py.

PDE library and fit. Candidates are wave U_tt=c2 U_xx, damped wave U_tt=-gamma U_t-c2 U_xx, heat U_t=D U_xx, advection-diffusion U_t=c U_x+D U_xx, reaction-diffusion U_t=D U_xx+k U, KdV U_t=-a U U_x-b U_xx, Burgers U_t=-a U U_x-D U_xx, a 5-term general wave U_tt=a U_xx+b U_t+c U+d U_x+e, and a 5-term general heat U_t=a U_xx+b U_x+c U+d U U_x+e. Each is fit by least squares (np.linalg.lstsq) over the common interior. Fit quality is rel_resid = ||resid||_2 / ||reference||_2 with reference the left-hand-side derivative; a 0.3 bar marks an acceptable fit.

Holdout protocol. For the wave equation we fitted c2 on a contiguous training time block and evaluated rel_resid on a disjoint test block (output/robustness.py: train[0,3] test(3,10], train[0,5] test(5,10], train[0,7] test(7,10], train[3,10] test[0,3)); output/analysis.py additionally uses a 60/40 split.

Fourier analysis. We took the rFFT in x, retained 99 modes with amp_max>0.1 (k~0.10..9.85), fit each mode's amplitude envelope log(amp)=log(A0)-gamma t, fit phase w by linear regression, and tested a linear dispersion w=c*k+d (output/analysis.py, output/results.json).

## Findings
### Finding 1: No candidate PDE fits the synthetic field to the 0.3 residual bar (null result)
- **Object and scope:** The dense synthetic field (101x201, t in [0,10], x in [-5,5]) as a single scalar field U(t,x); the question is whether one of the nine candidate PDEs holds.
- **Relationship or boundary:** The acceptance boundary is rel_resid < 0.3. No candidate crosses it; the best (5-term general wave) is 0.6835 and the pure wave is 0.8642. The residual does not collapse to a boundary effect; it is a global mismatch.
- **Quantitative result and analysis unit:** Per-candidate rel_resid over the common interior (n_points ~19208-19503): general_wave 0.6835; damped_wave 0.8628 (gamma=-0.0682, c2=-0.0800); wave 0.8642 (c2=0.079859); kdv 0.8818 (a=-0.7006, b=0.0617); burgers 0.8818; general_heat 0.8161 (a=0.1075); rxn_diff 0.9987 (D=-0.000282, k=0.04339); adv_diff 0.9998 (c=-0.000578, D=-0.002520); heat 0.9998 (D=-0.002516).
- **Evidence:** output/analysis.py (fit_pde, fit_linear) and output/results.json (synthetic.pde_fits); figures output/figures/fig1_synthetic_field.png and output/figures/fig3_modes.png.
- **Robustness:** Alternative stencils change c2 only modestly (central 0.079859, forward 0.073839, backward 0.072004, 5pt 0.075497; output/analysis.py alt_derivatives, corroborated by output/robustness_results.json wave_stencils_synthetic, where 5pt_central c2=0.075497, rel_resid 0.8682 and spectral_fft_x 0.9812) yet all remain rel_resid 0.87-0.98. Extra candidates U_t_6term (rel_resid 0.688; b_Uxxx=3.63e-15) and U_tt_3term (rel_resid 0.688; b_Uxxx=1.67e-14, d_U=-0.685) are no better. Fourier: 99 modes, linear dispersion w=c*k+d gives c=0.019067, d=0.351304, rel_resid 0.7687 (nonlinear dispersion); forcing f=U_tt-c2 U_xx has std 0.6529 and is neither separable nor proportional to U (f/U mean 2.21, std 36.77) nor U_xx (f/U_xx mean -1.04, std 105.67). Nonlinearity ratios U_t/U (std 253.76), U_t/U_x (std 30.90), U_t/U_xx (std 27.56) are large and x-dependent.
- **Counter-evidence, limitations, alternatives:** The single rel_resid scalar understates fit quality; a 5-term general form absorbs structure, so its 0.6835 is partly overfitting to a broad ansatz. Initial condition is U(0,x)=0 with an odd initial velocity (U_t(0) antisymmetric, max 0.049814), which launches a dispersive pulse rather than a simple wave, consistent with no closed-form PDE. We do not claim a PDE exists; the null is the result.
- **Falsification test:** Re-fit with a wider ansatz (e.g., a 3rd-derivative U_xxx dispersion term or a nonlinear U*U_xx term) on a denser/noisier realization and obtain rel_resid < 0.3, or observe the c2=1 residual shrink toward zero as a clean single PDE. A single candidate beating 0.3 on both training and holdout would falsify this null.

### Finding 2: The U_tt=U_xx (c2=1) residual is field-wide and interior-dominated, not a spatial-boundary artifact
- **Object and scope:** The residual field r(t,x)=U_tt-U_xx on the synthetic field, using central second differences, to localize where (if anywhere) the c2=1 wave equation fails.
- **Relationship or boundary:** The boundary is whether the failure concentrates at the spatial edges x=±5 (a boundary artifact) or throughout the domain. It is the latter: the spatial-boundary residual is exactly 0.000000 (U=0 at x=±5 for all t, and the residual is zeroed at those edges) while the interior mean |r| spans 0.64-3.60 across t (peak 3.598 at t=3.0), boundary/interior ratio = 0.000, and the residual peaks where the pulse amplitude is largest (t~3, U_max~1.5).
- **Quantitative result and analysis unit:** Overall r: min=-48.504, max=48.504, std=4.697. Fractions of all points: |r|>0.1 is 87.9% (17846/20301), |r|>0.5 is 45.9% (9310/20301), |r|>1.0 is 25.5% (5176/20301). Mean |r| by time: t=1.0:0.869, t=2.5:2.849, t=3.0:3.598 (peak), t=5.0:1.438. Max |r|=48.5 at t=5.9, x=4.9 (near +x edge).
- **Evidence:** output/analysis.py (compute_residual_field, boundary-concentration loop) and output/results.json (synthetic.residual_field_stats); figure output/figures/fig2_residual.png.
- **Robustness:** The conclusion is stencil- and split-invariant. All wave stencils show rel_resid 0.87-0.98 (3pt 0.8723, 5pt 0.8682, forward 0.8948, backward 0.8986, spectral 0.9812; output/robustness_results.json wave_stencils_synthetic). Holdout fits all fail (rel_resid_test 0.869-0.919; c2_train 0.0695-0.0819, output/robustness_results.json wave_holdout_splits), so the mismatch persists on unseen times. The c2 estimate is unstable across splits (0.064-0.082), indicating no stable wave speed.
- **Counter-evidence, limitations, alternatives:** The residual is dominated by large-amplitude pulse regions; a small-amplitude linear region might locally resemble a wave. The exact-zero boundary residual partly reflects U=0 at the edges (a Dirichlet-like condition), so the interior/exterior contrast is partly a consequence of that condition; it still shows the bulk of the field is not wave-like.
- **Falsification test:** Re-run the residual on a field generated from a known wave solution (or on a sub-window of low-amplitude, linear data) and show the residual concentrates at the edges or falls below the 0.3 bar in the interior. If the interior residual stays large and edge residual stays zero for an independently generated wave, this finding would be falsified.

### Finding 3: The sparse experimental field is best described by reaction-diffusion with positive growth, but at low confidence
- **Object and scope:** The sparse experimental cell-density field (5 time x 38 space x 3 channels, t=[0,48] h, x in [25,1875] step 50, source units), analyzed per channel and for channel-to-channel similarity.
- **Relationship or boundary:** The boundary is again rel_resid < 0.3. Reaction-diffusion is the best but still above the bar (0.41-0.54); heat, advection-diffusion, and wave are all poor (0.77-0.99). Confidence is limited by only five time points (coarse U_tt) and a sparse 38-point space grid; all D coefficients are in source-assay/h^2 units (no physical space unit).
- **Quantitative result and analysis unit:** Per-channel best (rxn_diff, D in source-assay/h^2, k in h^-1): ch0 D=31.07, k=0.0309, rel_resid 0.4078; ch1 D=51.45, k=0.0249, rel_resid 0.5039; ch2 D=37.61, k=0.0245, rel_resid 0.5376. For comparison ch0 wave c2=9.92 (0.8856), ch1 c2=15.90 (0.7743), ch2 c2=12.25 (0.9096); heat rel_resid 0.9966-0.9994; adv_diff 0.9914-0.9978. Mean log-linear growth rate (with intercept): ch0 0.029455/h, ch1 0.025233/h, ch2 0.025061/h. Peak values rise to ~0.0016 (ch0 0.000615->0.001594, ch1 0.000755->0.001650, ch2 0.000811->0.001650). Second-moment (M2) slope is negative (ch0 -1743, ch1 -3022, ch2 -2691 source-units^2/h) and peak trajectories are mostly at the left edge. Channels are similar but not identical: rel_diff ch0-vs-ch1 0.2115, ch0-vs-ch2 0.2294, ch1-vs-ch2 0.1513, max_diff ~0.0005; no clean spatial shift.
- **Evidence:** output/analysis.py (experimental per-channel fits) and output/robustness.py (M2, growth, peak trajectory); output/results.json (experimental.channels) and output/robustness_results.json (experimental_per_channel); figure output/figures/fig4_experimental.png.
- **Robustness:** The ranking is stable across the three channels (rxn_diff always best) but the absolute residual is high because of only five time points. The negative M2 slope and left-locked peaks argue against a simple symmetric diffusion picture and are counter-evidence to a clean reaction-diffusion solution. Channel rel_diff 0.15-0.23 means the replicates are not identical realizations.
- **Counter-evidence, limitations, alternatives:** With only 5 time samples, U_tt is a 1-point stencil in time and the fits are under-constrained; the rel_resid values should be treated as lower bounds on uncertainty. The negative "diffusion" coefficient (M2 slope) and the small peak displacements (|peak_b| ~0-43 units/h) are inconsistent with a propagating front. We therefore treat reaction-diffusion as a description of the sparse data, not a confirmed law.
- **Falsification test:** Add more time points (e.g., >=12) at the same channels and re-fit; if a different PDE (e.g., heat or a pure growth model without spatial diffusion) drops below 0.3 and the M2 slope becomes positive, the reaction-diffusion characterization is falsified. If rxn_diff stays best at rel_resid<0.3 with more points, the finding is strengthened, not falsified.

1. From the project root (or output/), run the core analysis: `python3 output/analysis.py` (writes output/results.json; requires numpy, json).
2. Run the robustness checks: `python3 output/robustness.py` (writes output/robustness_results.json).
3. Generate figures: `python3 output/generate_figures.py` (writes output/figures/*.png; figures may be absent if matplotlib is unavailable — the four PNGs above were produced when it was).

## Limitations
- Dense vs sparse: the synthetic field is dense (20,301 points) so PDE fits are well-posed; the experimental field has only 5x38x3 samples, making second-in-time derivatives coarse and all experimental PDE fits low-confidence.
- In-sample fits: all rel_resid values are computed on the same interior used for fitting (except the explicit holdout splits); the 0.3 bar is a heuristic, and no candidate clears it, so "no good PDE" is the honest conclusion.
- A single rel_resid scalar can understate or overstate fit quality (e.g., a broad general ansatz can overfit); stencil choice shifts c2 modestly (central 0.079859, 5pt 0.075497, spectral 0.011115) but none beat the 0.3 bar, so no stencil rescues the null.
- Fourier phase fits are noise-sensitive at low amplitude (phase_resid>1 for most high-k modes).
- Experimental coefficients are in source-assay units; no physical space or time unit is assumed, so "D" is not a physical diffusion constant.
- The two fields are not claimed to share one equation; the synthetic field is a dispersive pulse and the experimental field a slowly growing reaction-diffusion-like density, and the evidence supports treating them separately.
